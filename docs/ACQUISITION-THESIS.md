# The acquisition thesis — AMENDED AND RULED by the review board

**Status: ruled `amend` 2026-09-02** by a six-seat board under the owner's
standing delegation ("decisions are to be handled by the review board").
The amendments below are the board's own text, applied verbatim. Marketing phase 1 requires this before design
absorbs its constraints, and phase 3 (economics) cannot be computed without it.
The review board recorded it as unwritten on 2026-09-02; this is a draft written
from evidence already in the repo, not a decision. Overrule any line of it.

**Grounded in:** `docs/ecosystem/COMPETITIVE_AUTOMATION.md` (dated 2026-06-30,
owner-authored), and the Blink/Partiful reads in the project memory.

---

## The thesis, as one falsifiable sentence

> A host who has never planned this kind of event will, on seeing a single
> screen of a plan that was **derived from what their event is** rather than
> assembled from a template, understand within about ten seconds that the app
> already knows what their event needs — and that is the thing that makes them
> start one.

## The acquisition unit

**One screen: a real event's plan, mid-flight.** Not a landing page, not a
feature list, not a video of someone talking. The unit is the artifact the
product produces, shown at the moment it is most obviously *not* a blank
template — quantities sized to a real crowd, tasks dated off a real event date,
and a named next action.

This choice is a design constraint, not a preference, and it is the reason it
belongs at stage 2: **the screen has to be legible cold, at a glance, with no
sound and no caption.** If the plan only reads as derived once a human explains
it, the unit is wrong and the thesis fails with it.

## The single thing a stranger must understand

**"It already knew what this event needed before I told it."**

> **Amended by the board.** The original read *"It already knows what my event
> needs"* — present tense, and a claim the product does not yet keep on every
> path. The solemn path renders no ask at all (Decision 1), so *"always knows
> the next thing"* had no check behind it. The past tense also does the
> positioning work better: it locates the surprise at the moment of derivation
> rather than making an open-ended promise about the future.

Not "it's organized." Not "it has good templates." The competitive teardown's
own one-line is that everyone else automates *templates you author* or
*reminders off a date*, and this automates *the plan itself*. If that
distinction does not survive first contact, nothing downstream — pricing,
channel, creative — is standing on anything.

## What would falsify it

Any of these, and the thesis is wrong rather than under-executed:

1. **Strangers describe it in template language.** If unaided descriptions come
   back as "a checklist app", "a planner template", "like Trello for parties",
   the derived-plan distinction did not land, and the acquisition unit is not
   carrying the claim.
2. **They understand it but do not want it.** Comprehension and desire are
   different tests. If strangers state the proposition correctly and still would
   not start an event, the thesis is true and worthless, which is the same
   commercial outcome as false.
3. **They need the second screen.** If the unit only works as a sequence, it is
   not an acquisition unit — it is a demo, and demos do not survive a feed.
4. **They understand it as derived but attribute the derivation to a person or
   a service rather than the product.** Added by the board: comprehension that
   lands on "someone made this for them" is not comprehension of the product.

**Pre-registered threshold** — the board's replacement text, verbatim:

> Ten strangers see the unit for ten seconds; **the unit is then removed** and
> they describe in their own words what the product does. A response passes only
> if it entails that the plan was derived from this specific event's facts
> (date, crowd, kind) — **coded blind by two raters** against written criteria,
> disagreements resolved before unblinding. Threshold: **at least 6 of 10 pass,
> AND the unit must outscore a control arm** — the same screen with quantities
> and dates replaced by generic placeholders, shown to a separate 10 — **by at
> least 3.** Below either bar the thesis is refuted and the unit is redesigned
> rather than re-explained.

**Why the control arm.** The positioning seat: *6 of 10 is honest as a bar and
dishonest as a proof.* Without a control, a pass shows the unit is
**comprehensible** — not that it is **different**, which is the entire thesis.

**Why the unit is removed first.** Otherwise it tests reading comprehension of a
screen still in front of them.

Deciding these numbers after seeing results is not a test. They were written
down here first.

**Not yet pre-registered, flagged as a dissent:** the recruitment source.
Strangers drawn from event-planning communities will pass a template screen too.
Name the source before recruiting.

## What this forbids the build from doing

The part that pays, because it kills options now rather than at stage 9:

- **No acquisition unit may be mocked up.** It is built from the real surface or
  it is not the unit. A clip of a screen that does not exist is a promise.
- **The first screen must not require onboarding to be legible.** If the plan
  only looks derived after a host answers six questions, the unit cannot be the
  plan and the thesis needs rewriting, not the screenshot.
- **No claim about time saved, stress reduced, or events run** may ride the
  unit until something measures it. Those are the claims most likely to be
  reached for and least likely to have a check behind them.

## Sequencing — binding, from the board

**The amended hero card ships before the acquisition unit is shot.** The solemn
path renders no ask today, so the unit would depict a capability the code cannot
yet produce, and the threshold would run against a screen that does not exist.
The data-honesty seat put it as a precondition, not a preference: *the
empty-but-present ask state must exist in code before the marketing unit ships.*

The board named this the thesis's sternest test rather than its awkward
exception: if the product can state the one right thing to a grieving host
without selling, nudging or performing, then derivation-from-semantics is a real
capability and not a demo trick — **because the semantics of a repast are
precisely what a template cannot encode.**

## What it does NOT do

It does not authorize spend. Phase 3 — required revenue per paid host against a
named geography — is unwritten and unmeasured: **no acquisition cost has ever
been measured for this product.** Phase 1 clearing makes phase 2 runnable; phase
2 clearing makes phase 3 computable; only phase 3 clearing authorizes a dollar.
