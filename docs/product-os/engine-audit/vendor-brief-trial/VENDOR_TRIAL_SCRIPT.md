# Vendor Trial Script

## Message for a REAL vendor

> Hey — here's your event brief for the upcoming event. Please review it and
> use the button on the page to confirm or flag anything that needs to be
> corrected.
>
> [link]

## Message for a STAND-IN vendor

> Hey — I'm testing a vendor event brief for an event planning app I'm
> building.
>
> Can you open this link like you're the vendor for this event and respond
> using the button on the page?
>
> Please don't ask me what to do first — I'm testing whether the brief makes
> sense on its own.
>
> [link]

## Instructions for Todd

1. Mint the link from the vendor's cockpit row on a DISPOSABLE/demo event.
2. Send one of the messages above verbatim — no extra explanation.
3. Note the send time, channel, and whether it was link or QR.
4. Do not follow up for at least a few hours; chasing latency is part of what
   the loop is supposed to remove.
5. When the response arrives, apply the cockpit action(s) and screenshot
   before/after.
6. Fill the tracker row immediately while details are fresh.

## What to observe silently

- Did they open it on the first try? What device?
- How long did they read before acting?
- Did they scroll to their schedule cues? Did they mention the arrival time
  or load-in note (evidence they understood)?
- Which button did they press, and did they hesitate?
- Did they write a real note or leave it empty?
- Did they ask ANY question before responding? (Record it verbatim — every
  question is a confusion point.)

## What NOT to explain unless the vendor is fully blocked

- What the page is for, what "confirm" does, where the schedule is, whether
  they need an account (they don't), what happens after they submit.
- If you must unblock them, record exactly what you had to say and mark the
  row `understood_without_help = no`.

## Post-response questions (ask AFTER they submit)

1. In your own words, what was that page asking you to do?
2. Was anything confusing or missing?
3. Did anything on the page feel like it wasn't meant for you to see?
4. Would you rather have gotten this as a link, a text thread, or a phone
   call — and why?
5. (Real vendors) Did this save you a call or text you'd normally make?
