# Bands that their own commodity evidence does not support

**Date:** 2026-08-18 · Found while sourcing, not while looking for it
**Status:** measured and recorded. NOT fixed — changing a band is a product call.

## What this is

Grounding a priced row means checking that the cited figures actually bracket its
`unitCostRange`. Doing that across ~40 rows turned up **eleven** whose band the
evidence does not support. They are listed here rather than papered over with a
citation, because a source that does not support the number is worse than no
source: it makes an unverified figure look checked.

## The pattern

The two directions are not random and they do not cancel out.

**Drinks and prepared items read high.** The likeliest explanation is a purchase
CHANNEL no source covers: a single-serve bottle rather than a 12-pack, a catered
tray rather than raw meat. If so the bands are right and the sources are simply
pricing a different thing — but nothing in the corpus says so, so a reader cannot
tell that from an over-estimate.

**Snacks read low.** Chips are a genuinely expensive commodity by weight and the
bands appear to be intuition rather than shelf prices.

That split — the same author estimating one category high and another low — is
what makes this worth surfacing rather than silently correcting.

## What was NOT done, deliberately

- **No band was changed.** These numbers drive host budgets. Moving them is a
  product decision with an event pro in the room, not a side effect of a
  sourcing pass.
- **No citation was attached to any row above.** Citing sources that contradict
  the band would convert an honest estimate into a false claim, which is the one
  outcome worse than leaving it unsourced.
- Where a row was groundable with an honest caveat, it WAS grounded and the
  caveat is in the claim — `graduation:67` says its ceiling holds only because
  cheap components outweigh the chips by volume, and the Kwanzaa corn row says
  its band is a December price against summer sources.

## The ask

Each row needs one of two answers, and only a person who knows the product can
give it:

1. **The band is right** — it prices a channel (single-serve, prepared, catered)
   the sources do not cover. Then the row needs a source for THAT channel, and
   the claim should name it.
2. **The band is wrong** — it is an estimate that shelf prices now contradict.
   Then it should move, and the sourcing above is what it should move to.
