01

One fact, end to end

The app's hardest problem is not computing things. It is keeping track of who said each thing — because the same field holds a number the host typed and a number the app invented, and only one of them may be repeated to a caterer.

startTimeSourceA derived 3pm never reaches an invite or a vendor brief.startTimeIsConfirmed
guestCountSourceOur typical headcount never reaches a vendor.guestCountIsConfirmed
choiceSourceThe app may not sign the host's name to its own pick.choiceAttribution
bracket ruleAn unfilled [blank] never reaches a guest — withheld, not repaired.guestSafeText

Four instances of one shape: record who said it at the moment it is decided, and gate the outward surfaces on that record. Each was built after the same defect was found live on a different field.

02

Four producers, one list

Nothing in the app decides what to do first. Four independent producers each push what they think matters into one array, and a comparator settles it afterwards. This is why “four engines give four different answers” was a finding rather than a bug — it is the architecture.

04

Which half a host can reach

The engines are shared; the surfaces are not. A large, well-specified body of vendor intelligence is reachable only from the frozen shell, and the deletion date is real.

05

How a claim earns its way onto the screen

Two rules do most of the work of keeping the app honest, and both were written after a green test suite shipped a lie.

An unknown fact refuses its rule

Facts are {value, known}. A rule that needs a headcount and does not have one does not compare against zero and quietly succeed — it declines to fire. This is why a 4-guest event and a 400-guest event stopped receiving the same recommendation, and why a missing number can never masquerade as a small one.

Withhold, never repair

When the app cannot honestly say something, it says nothing and tells the host why. Stripping the brackets out of “Guests can park [street / driveway]” leaves a sentence that means something different and is still wrong. A guest shown no parking line is strictly better than a guest shown our placeholder — and the host, not the app, is the one who can fix it.

What this page does not claim

Grounding is uneven and the split is worth knowing: 532 of 622 priced food lines pass a registry-checked provenance predicate against 297 dated sources — while the per-head bands that produce the headline budget, and 224 vendor cost ranges, have no provenance field in their shape at all. The system was built for the grocery list and has not yet reached the largest numbers a host sees.

Every figure on this page was measured against the working tree on 18 September 2026 — counts by direct enumeration, engine behaviour by executing the real functions, reachability from the import graph. Nothing is recalled or estimated. The two module counts are deliberately not a ratio: 172 is the top level of src/lib, while the 139 the shell imports include modules from its subdirectories.
