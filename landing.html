The system — how it thinks

One source of truth, one next action.

Every surface in Event Boss reads from the same engines — the readiness bar, the decision board, and the hero can never disagree. When it says you're in good shape, you actually are.

01

The hero never flatters you

If three decisions are past their window, the top of your screen says so — and takes you to the first one. “You're in good shape” is a claim the system has to earn, checked against every open essential before it's allowed to say it.

One-source hero
02

Assigned is not handled

If Marcus is bringing the ice, your next action isn't “buy ice” — it's “confirm with Marcus.” Helpers get a real status ladder: assigned, confirmed, brought. The backup stays in your plan until the confirm actually happens.

Helper responsibility
03

The plan knows what day it is

Readiness means readiness for the phase you're in. Planning gaps retire on event day; the day before, fourteen cards compress into one honest page — what still matters, what to confirm, what to stop worrying about.

Phase truth
04

Every tap lands somewhere real

Every button routes to the exact row it names — the food line, the vendor, the rain-plan field — and the target opens itself when you arrive. Dead buttons are treated as defects and tested against a hundred scenarios in CI.

The refusals — what it will not do

Trust is a feature. It's built out of refusals.

No fake AI

Nothing is invented.

No fabricated vendor lists, no imaginary prices, no “insights” without a source. If the app doesn't know, it says so and shows you where to put the answer.

No guilt engine

Calm by default.

One cue at a time. Settled work folds itself away. If you chose not to track RSVPs, it will never nag you about replies.

No leaks

Your people stay private.

Helper names never appear in vendor briefs or guest messages. What the vendor sees is a whitelist, not a data dump.
