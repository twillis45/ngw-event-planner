Under the hood — the quiet intelligence

It doesn't just track your plan. It understands your event.

Cultural

It knows what this table wants.

A Juneteenth cookout gets asked about the red drink and the red velvet. A crab feast gets bushel math. A repast gets a gentler voice. Offered when it fits — never assumed, never pushed.

Human

It knows who the day is for.

The guest of honor, their song, the one moment you said can't be missed — it keeps the heart of the event on the schedule, not just the chores around it.

Location

It knows where you are.

Prices tuned to your city, stores and help near you, directions that actually land people at your door.

Weather

It watches your exact sky.

The forecast for your spot, read against your plan — outdoor event, no backup, rain on Thursday? You hear about it while there's still time to be calm about it.

Venue

It knows a backyard from a hall.

Home means kitchen, driveway, and neighbors. A venue means load-in, contacts, and house rules — and your vendors get exactly the piece they need.

RSVP

Replies become the plan.

Share one link; the yeses roll in. Twelve more said yes? The ribs, the seats, and the ice resize themselves. And if you'd rather just set a number — it will never once nag you about replies.

Communication

It keeps the thread.

Invites, updates, the friendly nudge to the three who haven't answered — drafted for you, sent when you say, with honest delivery status. “Delivered” means delivered, never guessed.

Dietary

It remembers who can't eat what.

Your cousin's allergy lives on the plan, not in your memory — flagged on every dish it touches, with a real vegetarian main sized to the actual count.

Timing

It knows the windows.

Ice is a morning-of buy. The caterer needed three weeks. Every decision has an easy window — it tells you before one closes, not after.

Vendors

Quoted isn't booked.

It tracks what's actually locked — deposit, arrival time, paperwork — and drafts the “still on for Saturday?” note, so you're never calling the DJ at nine in the morning.

Money

It keeps honest count.

Every dollar traces to your plan and your prices — spent is spent, planned is planned, nothing quietly rounded into good news.

Memory

It's already remembering this year for next.

What ran out, what was the hit, which call you'd make again — captured as you go, so next year's gathering doesn't start from a blank page.
